Details.

Those are quite a necessity to Outer Wilds. I say this for many things, such as music, sound design, nonlinearity, etc. I believe this is because Outer Wilds is just such a unique game in almost every aspect of game design.

But to achieve that myself, I must delve into each. Today, I think I’ll think about details. Specifically, two things.

#### Story Details

As mentioned before, things like the testing in Hollow Lantern or the statue workshop on Giant’s Deep are part of what makes Outer Wilds feel like Outer Wilds. The ability for one thing to correlate to so many others without any seemingly obvious relationship.

I really do wonder about their thinking process behind this moon. I believe they simply decided on one thing with Brittle Hollow. It has a moon that shoots asteroids that causes it to break apart, and then they build from there to create puzzles using that base mechanism.

But making it volcanic also to test the strength of the material found in Timber Hearths core (another correlation) is just something I can’t wrap my head around. Realistically it was just a great coincidence, as I’ve found can happen many times when making a story. But still, it’s a great detail.

---

But anyways, besides Hollow Lantern and any other celestial bodies, you need smaller details. Human scale, or in this case Hearthian scale.

One idea I had about this was a small room in the LOC, or at least one of the existing places that I’ve yet to think about. Of course, the final experiment is really the only case of sending anything back through time in the Expansion, but I wouldn’t doubt they used it only once for quantum information travel.

Anyways, the idea was that they had sent an object through the wormhole pair before waiting so that the △T (NTI) of the wormholes was less than the length of time the wormholes were open. Of course, this means that the exit the object comes from didn’t exist at the time.

Now there are a few cases of what happens here. One is that the object simply ceases to exist within the timeline. But that’s no fun. The second case is that an exit simply forms relative to where it was at the time of entry. This is my preferred case.

Why? Because it allows me to use Dark Bramble, and Dark Bramble is fun. If the relative spatial position of the white hole in the LOC was located at the same place Dark Bramble was at t - △T, then the object would end up inside the non-Euclidean space of the planet. This of course assumes the Solar System moves, as a planet would never cross Ember Twin’s path in a stable orbit, at least in the 22-minute timeframe.

It’s small with no real importance, but I believe this serves as an example as to why these small details can make the game feel never-ending. Always something more to find.

I’m unsure as to how to hint towards this object being inside the planet, however. My first idea was to have a small quantum signal since the LOC has so many quantum things inside of it, but assimilated objects don’t seem to give off a quantum signal. Maybe that is a deeper behavior of quantum objects that would take longer to assimilate.

My second idea was to make a mural, but again, this object is not of the utmost importance, so of course I shouldn’t spend a whole lot of a time with it.

But again, things like this both increase the gameplay time and add more paths when you’re stuck (which really is a main goal here with these details). That choice of where to go when you’re stuck is why these paths, whether important or not, can lead to a greater understanding of the whole story.

Perhaps this object, whatever it is, can provide an explanation for a certain behavior, or very subtly hint towards something else. But of course, I don’t want it to say much, it’s still mostly for the jokes. It’s a balancing act, making the player think this object is important and spending so long only to find something mundane can either cause frustration or more immersion. I’m trying to decide whether you could simply find it on your travels and have that “Aha” moment or deliberately try to search for it.

If I were to decide deliberation, then it would have to mean something more than a simple cup or something. The Quantum path, for example, doesn’t really explain much in terms of finding the Eye, but it certainly gives more context and hints towards other paths (from what I remember).

I could maybe expand it into something more than a mundane object, an actual puzzle. Just something. I’m unsure.

---

Anyways, the point is that story details and sidelines such as this (well, maybe not this exactly) give the players more opportunities to continue when they’re stuck. It also increases gameplay time and story depth.

I also do this because I don’t want the entire story of The Vestige to rest on Ember Twin and Brittle Hollow. They’re already very populated planets (as that’s where the crash site of the two Escape Pods was).

Including planets like Giant’s Deep, Dark Bramble (everyone loves Dark Bramble), and Timber Hearth is just better, even if it’s not entirely dependent on them.

#### Gameplay details

While not entirely too separate from story details, gameplay details give the story more immediate depth. Story details take time for connections to be made, which is something you really do have to think about as a designer.

Kind of off topic here, but that’s one of my main hurdles to a one-person team so far. It’s easy to get biased into thinking something is too easy or too hard simply because you’re the one who made it. The connection between everything is already there, though it can be completely niche and not obvious to most players.

So, you practically have to make things like 50% more obvious than you think is necessary to get that same level of recognition. I believe that is a very important concept.

Of course, I don’t know any of this. Almost every single thing about game design for this story has been based off of my own experiences playing both Outer Wilds and various other games, along with designing the story itself. I’ve yet to have an actual tester for it because, well, I haven’t been able to find one, and I don’t actually have anything at the moment.

Of course by tester, I could also mean just looking at what I have written to see if it makes sense. But it’s not exactly the same because it’s written out for you. I have to translate that story into actual gameplay, which is one of the biggest struggles I’ve had in this mod so far. The three laws of persistence can make sense written out, but it can make absolutely no sense at all, even in hindsight, if the gameplay isn’t really made right.

I know I overthink a ton of these things, mostly just because I like writing about the game, but it’s a real concern to have, I believe.
