While I don’t have a ton of time right now (20 minutes or so), I do want to talk about a small thing I’ve been thinking about this morning.

A poem from Robert Frost in 1st period:

“Nature’s first green is gold,

Her hardest hue to hold.

Her early leaf’s a flower;

But only so an hour.

Then leaf subsides to leaf.

So Eden sank to grief,

So dawn goes down to day.

Nothing gold can stay.”

Now, quite notably, this is a normal poem about the impermanence of good things in life, or life itself being an impermanent thing. It applies to many things.

But by my obsessive and curious nature, it made me think about Outer Wilds (surprising, I know). But that’s exactly the point of the game. No matter your choices, your efforts to try and preserve the goodness of the universe you call home are in vain as it comes to its natural end.

That’s what the ending subjectively tells you, at least. The fleeting nature of the star system, the unimportance and lack of specialty of the hatchling’s specifically, is one of the core game mechanics and emotional distinctions throughout the entire game.

“Echoes of the Eye” explores exactly this. This species wanted to preserve their golden universe full of life. Their short-sightedness and nostalgia forced them to create a fake version of this, guilted that their decision to destroy a place they loved ended in vain.

But that’s not what we’re focusing on.

As mentioned before, the hatchling and their star system are not important in the grand scheme of things. The star is dying, along with every other. Not because the hatchling had an important plotline to fulfill.

The Nomai could’ve just as easily done the same if the coincidence of the Interloper had not ended their story.

The point of the Eye is not that the hatchling had “activated” the birth of a new universe and thus killing the old one. The universe was already dying. It is simply the hatchling that had provided the conscious ability to collapse the probability of the new universe.

The point is coming to terms with this, accepting that life is fleeting, and that you have to make the best of it while it’s here. Everyone has their natural end, even the universe itself. But the lingering echo of your actions, the choices you made and the impacts you had on friends and family, can linger on far after you, or the universe, is gone.

This is shown in the credits of course. A group of creatures surrounding a campfire, telling stories of a strange ancient scout older than the universe itself.
