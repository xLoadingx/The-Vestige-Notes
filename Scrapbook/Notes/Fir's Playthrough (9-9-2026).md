So, I've started watching [Fir's playthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mCZfz-WtSOk) of the game and I've somewhat learned three things, one of which I kinda already knew.

## Players are not omnipotent (obviously)
Not really much to say about this one.

Players do not possess the same thinking process or mental map of the game/mod as the, well, author does. As I mentioned before, you have to make things a lot more obvious then you think you do, because players can easily miss big clues that, to you, are practically spoilers.

It's kind of hard to wrap my head around, especially when actually trying to make things. I think it might be related to the fact that I'm trying to recreate the feeling of the base game in my mod.

And by "recreate the feeling," I guess, I'm accidentally trying to recreate it for myself. Which is not possible, if I'm the one making it, of course. But in that process, I'm making it hard for me to play through it (which gives that feeling, somewhat) but in turn it makes it almost impossible for people who... are not me.

## Outer Wilds starts... oddly linear
I haven't seen the start of Outer Wilds in a long time.

Outer Wilds starts weirdly linear, which is a problem I recently had where I felt the start of my mod was far too linear. Now of course mine was a lot worse, but, still, I find it interesting:

1. You wake up, talk to Slate, tells you to go to the Observatory.
2. In that process, you can talk to everyone else, gain context of what the heck you're doing, etc. The Hearthians know the hatchling as someone who has prepared for a long time for this flight. The player, however, started 5 minutes ago.[^1]
3. You get to the Observatory. This is where it somewhat diverges, since there's so many things you can read about in here. Specifically, one of these is the Nomai text on the wall, with the Eye symbol (see [[Fir's Playthrough (9-9-2026)#Do not fear to give vital information|Vital Information]]) states that the new locator has been built on Attlerock.
4. You get the launch codes, leave, and then the statue does its thing. Confusing, but oddly enough, a lot of players don't pay much mind to neither it nor the death screen.[^2]
5. You go to the ship, and then you can... pretty much go anywhere, but a lot of people go to Attlerock because it's both closest and was mentioned in the Observatory.
6. From there, you can find the locator, and it mentions the Eye of the Universe, along with different planets and honestly quite linear "go here" messages from the Nomai, though to be fair it was early in the timeline there.

## Do not fear to give vital information
Now this one is also strange because the developers clearly had no fear of giving such vital structures of the game to the player so early. This demonstrates a clear understanding of player ignorance, which I find nice!

Quite literally, in the observatory, you can find a piece of Nomai wall with the Eye on the Universe symbol on it. This is quite wild to me looking back, but it also reinforces the fact that pulling stuff from the start to be referenced at the end can be a strong emotional hook.

Players do not know what this symbol is of course, it simply invokes curiosity, as do most things in this game. What was so odd to me though, in hindsight, was the Attlerock Eye Locator. They just do not refrain from saying "Eye of the Universe," which is kind of weird to me from a game design perspective, because... the Eye is the whole point of the game.

They do this a lot, apparently, which is interesting to note.

---

But the thing about all of this, is that Outer Wilds game design, and The Vestige game design, is not the same. I feel like I've been treating it like that.

Because The Vestige is not a standalone game, it is a part of the Outer Wilds world. And people who are playing this mod, I assume, have already beaten the game. They already understand Outer Wilds, and all of it's connections and what it means.

And that makes it tough... to recreate that feeling of curiosity, or, some other name I've been unable to pinpoint. Because you can only create curiosity through new things, unlike Outer Wilds, which has an abundance of things to connect other things to.

And that might sound like "Just make more things" but you have to understand that The Vestige is not Outer Wilds 2. It does not *need* the same amount of content to do similar, no, but if you really want to apply the same design as Outer Wilds, you're gonna need a lot of connections.

And the problem with that is that if you connect your story to what already exists in Outer Wilds (a good idea for realistic stories!), they already know how that connects. It does not invoke curiosity because they can already see how that relationship works. They find the modded part that connects it to the base part, but, they already know the base part.

And that's kind of where this diverges in design-language. You have to think from the player's perspective.

For an Outer Wilds developer, they know literally nothing. You can tell them anything, as long as it works with everything else, which indeed is tough within itself.

But for me, and other mod developers, the player has already woken up here many times before. They do not have any interests apart from new modded things. Because, think about it, the player goes in *expecting* modded stuff. They know what is modded.

A new player to the game sees all this weird alien stuff and a Earth-like planet with other planets in the sky. That is a huge source of curiosity, especially seeing that cannon break when you wake up, and all other Hearthians pretending they didn't see anything.

A new player to the *mod* goes in already knowing Timber Hearth. They already know what the Eye means, what it looks like, how it behaves. They are focusing for modded stuff. And that makes it a lot harder because they will naturally be harsher critics. Of course this isn't fully true, people can appreciate a good Outer Wilds mod even if they don't think "Huh, I can't tell if that's modded." because obviously it is, no matter how good it fits into the Outer Wilds design language.

So, yeah, it's just... tough.
