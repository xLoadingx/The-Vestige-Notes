Outer Wilds mostly actually only has a single main story line, despite being such a non-linear game.

What I mean by story line is what is necessary to explore to beat the game or (broadly) understand what happened. Take the Statue workshop as an example. While technically it is connected to the ATP in the form of one of the warp towers (I believe) and where the Statue’s were originally built, the game could totally go without it without affecting the actual story.

What I mean is that a lot of things outside “Get the core and find the Vessel” path of the game could be removed without much adjustment. Yes, it would make the story almost entirely feel incomplete, as I’m not saying it’d make a great game, I’m just saying that outside of that story line, most other things are either to gain knowledge about the main story line or extra detail to make the story feel more coherent.

I believe that’s something I’m somewhat missing. I think I’m trying too hard to make everything important to things like the LOC or the Shard. What makes Outer Wilds feel so great is there’s almost always more to explore, even if you find everything important. There’s so many little details and implications that I just love.

Of course this isn’t saying that I believe the story of The Vestige is flawed. I just believe it is not down to the details. This is most likely because I simply am early in the process and am focusing on the core story line (like the base game ATP and Vessel stuff).

There’s many examples to choose from talking about this. I’m quite surprised of how well the Outer Wilds devs can accomplish this with almost everything so well. Here’s a few:

On the volcanic moon of Brittle Hollow (which serves as both a location of interest and a reason for Brittle Hollow collapsing), you can find testing of the material found in Timber Hearth’s core in the lava, with the Nomai surprised at its amazing durability.

This is interesting because such a simple moon serves so many purposes that lead to completely unrelated things. The superheated meteorites that are launched from the moon are what causes Brittle Hollow to break apart. That serves as both an environmental hazard and a way to get into the Tower of Quantum Knowledge, something you wouldn’t think is related. While the detail of Nomai testing out the material is completely unneeded, it simply makes the story feel more complete all around the solar system.

Another example is the White Hole Station. It’s connected to the black hole in Brittle Hollow, made for the Nomai to get back to the Black Hole Forge after accidentally falling into the black hole. However, it also serves as the entry point for the Negative Time Interval (I believe) followed up by the High-Energy Lab which led to the entire idea of the Ash Twin Project in the Southern Observatory for their original goal of finding the Eye but not having a way to do it in a reasonable amount of time.

As you can see, it usually escalates fast, or at least always branches off from that main spine of Outer Wilds.

As a last example, the Vessel. Similarly to The Vestige, the story both (somewhat) starts and ends with the Vessel. This is an unintentional mirror, but anyways. The Vessel is what the Nomai originally crashed in Dark Bramble with. It’s needed to beat the game with the Advanced Warp Core from the Ash Twin Project (which was developed in the Black Hole Forge on Brittle Hollow) along with the coordinates found from the Probe Tracking Module at the center of Giants Deep.

And in order to learn how to reach the interior of Giants Deep to learn the coordinates, you have to travel deep into Dark Bramble in order to find Feldspar, who leads you to learn how he figured it out (though indirectly because Feldspar is just like that).

All in all, it’s very impressive how interconnected everything is, and that’s a very important aspect in making a story feel like Outer Wilds. Currently, I do not have that. I currently only have the equivalent of entering the Ash Twin Project and finding out they failed due to the unfortunate circumstances with the Interloper, but nothing like Hollow’s Lantern or the Statue Workshop and how that influences the main story line in a meaningful way.

Of course, again, I’m still quite “early” in the design process and am still working on the main spine in the first place. I do know that getting that main spine correct before working on any of the smaller details is usually a good idea, but I can’t help but feel bad for not doing so. Perhaps it’s just because the details are what I love and I want to be able to make my own.

However, my main problem right now is I’m not exactly sure what that would be. The main story line can support itself, or at least should, somewhat similar to the way the game goes from the Vessel to the Ash Twin Project (and then back to the Vessel).

I go from the fragment scene on the North Pole of Brittle Hollow to the Expansion of the LOC and then back to the fragment scene on Brittle Hollow. Or at least, that’s one of the endings I have in mind, the other is also at the Vessel, though I feel a lot of mods do that (at least ones like Astral Codec that influence the scene at the Eye) so I’ll have to do it in a unique manner.

Of course I’m not saying I have to do any of this just to be unique from other mods, only because I believe it’s what puts a spotlight on Outer Wilds as such a unique and emotional. While I may not be able to get the exact emotional punches with the argument and Eye scene as I’d want (it feels somewhat disappointing to reach an emotional end without much reason to care), I believe I can still apply this and the other things I’ve learned working on this project to make it as close as I can so other people can feel the same.

Two of the only examples I can think of that aren’t like this are the Quantum story line and the Echoes of the Eye DLC. The Quantum story line I believe is intentionally split off from the actual story line (though it does still use parts of the story such as Brittle Hollow breaking for the Tower of Quantum Knowledge).

Its only purpose is to serve as extra explanation for the behavior of the Eye and why it does what it does. While it doesn’t explain the exact process as to why it creates a new universe or survives it in the first place, it does show how that universe is chosen or in what kind of form it’s created in (the probability sphere).

Echoes of the Eye is also intentionally separate as it’s a DLC rather, it was never meant to be fully part of canon (while they did change some text to allow for it in the base game, the story works perfectly fine without it, hence the DLC aspect). It, while a huge expansion, only explains as to why the Eye’s signal went away.

This is what the Vestige should (and has) to be, really. It has to have a core story line that should not fully depend on these small details apart from the fact that it makes the main story line more complex though still understandable in a way that feels like there’s always one more detail you missed.

And I have that main story line for the most part, but I’m not sure what else outside of that (the equivalent of my Statue Workshop and Hollow’s Lantern) would be. Its not like I’m trying to make Outer Wilds again, no, it’s much smaller than that, but it still has to be nonlinear. That’s one of the major points as to why Outer Wilds is so fun.

And that’s possible, though the hard part is making them all related and converging the players into the same ending(s) that makes sense with all paths. That’s what I believe is the difficult part, along with actually coming up with these details as well.

What exactly those could be for the Vestige? I’m not sure, as I don’t think there’s much I have currently or anything I could think of outside of the LOC, Brittle Hollow, and Ember Twin. Sure, it’s related quite heavily to the ATP story line but I want there to be more to explore throughout the solar system apart from those two planets.

Dark Bramble, maybe, because all players obviously love Dark Bramble and I think it’d be fun. But what about the Vestige could possibly go into Dark Bramble? The thing is, I obviously know not to force anything, but trying to imagine a place where a detail could go before the reasoning behind it sometimes allows you to come up with fun ideas.
