One of the main problems I have with the story right now is how to enter the Expansion. Specifically, this is one part I worried about being too close to mirroring the base game.

The problem with this is that I can't copy the design of the ATP tower puzzle since that just wouldn't really make sense here and that's just blatantly not a great mystery. But another big problem is that I can't just make it because it needs a puzzle, the Nomai need a reason to have made it this way.

Considering the LOC requires quite a tight seal, this quite broadens the possibilities of what I can do for the entrance, since all I need is a way to get in without physically traveling through an entrance (similar to the ATP).

The Expansion is quite late game despite simply being locked behind early-game ignorance, similarly to pretty much everything else in the game. While the ATP directly doesn't actually tell you much if you enter it early, it's definitely not intended to be entered long before visiting things like the Black Hole Forge.

So, I've never really came up with a great solution for this yet. Here are a few ideas I've came up with though, and one I might actually go with if I can adjust it a little bit:

#### Warp Pad
One of my first ideas was to do something similar to the ATP puzzle. Though this idea didn't get far considering the original LOC isn't on the equator and there's no reliable way to get it to point towards the shared center of the Hourglass twins.

To solve this, I thought of using a gimbal of sorts, but then again that doesn't really create much of a puzzle considering how much the Expansion reveals. Astral Codec also uses this on Dark Bramble which is presumably where I subconsciously thought of this, though I don't want to copy it nor do I think it's a really good idea.

And it's for the same reason as the ATP, so, it's just a copy.
#### Some form of light trigger
There's a bit of a strange sequence when you enter the LOC where you have to travel back and forth between the controls and the LOC fairly often.[^1]

I thought, since you have to do this a ton already anyways, to use the light toggles as some form of quantum entanglement. Though, I didn't exactly come up with any idea to make it harder than just standing on a rock.

I could not figure that out unfortunately, since it has to be realistic but hard (realistic because Nomai, hard because game design).

However, this does kind of lead into the next idea of psychological difficulty instead of gameplay difficulty.

#### Quantum Entangled Hallway
Simple ignorance can go a long way, and I'm usually quite surprised at the ignorance of players, most likely because a core implication in this game is to be obvious in hindsight.

The entrance can be very simple, and this is the idea I'm still thinking about.

There's a simple dead end hallway in the original LOC that just has a bit of decoration. When you first enter the LOC, you can't really see anything apart from your flashlight, only to explore a bit of what's going on inside of here.

I'm unsure of whether you should know about the controls yet here. Probably not since there's an intentional "stuck" point right after looking around the LOC where it branches off into currently unknown paths.

Anyways, this hallway is quantum with another practically identical hallway that's located in the Expansion. Whether they have any meaningful difference between them, I'm not sure of yet.

So when you step inside and toggle your flashlight, you simply get transported between them (as they swap places).

My explanation right now for why it's so complicated is because:
1. It's a quantum/information control research lab. They made the hallway this way simply as another experiment.
2. It's a legitimately good way to protect the Expansion from outside light.
3. They wanted to have fun lmao

As for why they didn't do that with the ATP? Uhhh well that's something I haven't answered yet.

The only sequence-skipping problem I can see with this so far is if the player enters the hallway during their first look-through (which is absolutely plausible), thus triggering the quantum object to change places. Perhaps I have a misunderstanding of quantum behavior, and they'd need to turn their flashlight off first. Some players might even do that in this seemingly unrelated hallway, which is unsolvable, but, who knows.
