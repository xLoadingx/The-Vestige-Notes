#Phenomenon 
## Base Game Behavior

The macroscopic effects of quantum behavior in Outer Wilds are condensed into 3 rules in-game:
1. Rule of Quantum Imaging
	Observing an image of a quantum object is the same as observing the object itself.
 ^4e36ba
2. Rule of Quantum Entanglement
	 When an observer is in contact with a quantum object and they are not observing their environment, they move along with the quantum object.

3.  Rule of the Sixth Location
	The Quantum Moon is the Eye's moon. The Quantum Shrine must be on the north pole to reach the 6th location.

Observation of an object collapses its macroscopic uncertainty. This allows a quantum object to appear at one of its probabilistic locations when reobserved, as long as it's new location is not in view of any observer or in the state of another quantum object.

>SOLANUM: There is fundamental uncertainty throughout the universe. Normally, this uncertainty is only observable on a very small scale. As one approaches the Eye, however, that uncertainty grows _enormously_.

This implies that quantum uncertainty (similar to realistic physics) is found throughout the entire universe, though only at very small scales. As you approach the Eye (or anything that has [[Persistence#Law of Assimilation|assimilated]] its ruleset), you can find that uncertainty increases to the scale of unusual macroscopic behaviors.

## Information

I believe that the rule of Quantum Imaging can be expanded upon to include that any observation about the state of a quantum object is the same as observing the object itself. This is due to the fact that conscious observation is based less on visibility of the object itself, and more visualization of state within a conscious observer. 

This comes from the way quantum entanglement works. Even with the lights off, a quantum object is still considered observed, but rather it's *state* instead is what is not observed, meaning it is not forced to collapse into a specific one.

This creates two unique cases for the [[Low-Light Observation Chamber#Final Experiment|Final Experiment]]:

1. The Quantum Object found within the center can be found at the predicted location due to its future state being sent back in time by the Negative Time Interval, thus making it constrained to be at that position because of causality.
2. The Quantum Object found within the center can be found at the predicted location due to the displayed information truthfully describing the object's future state, and is forced to collapse to that position.

Which one of these apply specifically to the Final Experiment? Perhaps both.

Quantum Objects are inherently affected by Information being perceived by a conscious observer. While all matter within the universe is affected by Information and vice-versa, quantum objects apply that to a macroscopic effect.
